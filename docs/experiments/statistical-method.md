# Fixed-horizon experiment method

Initial metric: server-confirmed trial per unique eligible exposed assignment. One visitor hash per experiment; one company and one assignment per trial link. Activation uses the existing seven-day first-project business milestone. Paid is retained as downstream diagnostics; it is not a second winner test. No lifetime legacy rates enter this method.

Exactly two arms, independent 50/50 assignment. PostgreSQL draws the slot once per assignment. Reused approved content receives fresh experiment and arm UUIDs. Eligible exposure is first visible document exposure received by the server during the enrollment horizon. Preview/internal/known-bot traffic is excluded, with explicit imperfect-human-identification caveat. Browser IDs approximate visitors, not people.

Predeclare an evidence-backed baseline p0 and absolute MDE d in probability units (0.01 means one percentage point), baseline source, sample per arm, enrollment dates, conversion window and activation non-inferiority margin. There is no default OPS baseline. The per-arm planning formula is ceil((z(.975)*sqrt(2*pbar*(1-pbar))+z(.8)*sqrt(p0*(1-p0)+(p0+d)*(1-p0-d)))^2/d^2). It matches the normal approximation of the two-sided pooled two-proportion z test. An illustrative 2% to 3% comparison requires 3826 per arm; these numbers are test fixtures, not measured OPS conversion.

Decision is attempted only after enrollment end + conversionWindowDays + qualityWindowDays. Each trial must occur from first eligible exposure (inclusive) to exposure + conversionWindowDays (exclusive), also before assignment expiry. Quality window is anchored to each canonical trial start; at least seven days to observe the canonical activation milestone. Enrollment never extends itself to chase significance. Trials received late retain their original timestamps; a completed decision stores a frozen cohort snapshot and cannot pool into a new experiment.

SRM checks assignment counts against 50/50 using Pearson chi-square df=1 (equivalent two-sided normal at sqrt(chi-square)), threshold p<.001. Exposed counts also undergo that check as collection/render quality. Collection loss makes the experiment invalid. No exposure is not equivalent to no data collection failure. The health worker and runtime logs must prove collection operational before any result is trusted.

Primary test: pooled-null standard error, two-sided alpha=.05. Each arm must contain at least ten trials and ten non-trials; otherwise the normal approximation is not used and result is inconclusive. Zero/zero and insufficient samples cannot win. Report absolute difference, p-value, and unpooled 95% Wald effect interval. A significant harm retains the control; absence of significant difference is inconclusive.

A candidate trial lift must also pass a predeclared activation-per-visitor non-inferiority guardrail: one-sided 95% lower bound above negative absolute margin. Sparse activation outcomes are inconclusive. This is an intersection requirement for promotion, not a second route to declaring a winner. No engagement score/model rationale can override it.

Finite integer named cohort fields, valid clock, finite planning parameters, and finite computed uncertainty are checked. These approximations are intentionally conservative in sparse samples; the engine will not substitute an unreviewed alternative test. Repeated human previews of the numbers do not permit early stopping. Allocation, exclusions, attribution coverage and collection losses must be reviewed before enablement.

Primary references: [statsmodels two-proportion z test](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportions_ztest.html), [normal-test power parameters](https://www.statsmodels.org/dev/generated/statsmodels.stats.power.NormalIndPower.solve_power.html), [Microsoft sample-ratio validation](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/diagnosing-sample-ratio-mismatch-in-a-b-testing/). The exact planning formula is stated above because Cohen arcsine effect-size calculators can produce a different approximate sample requirement.
